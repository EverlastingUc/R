# Rosenblatt (1958)

## Citation

Frank Rosenblatt. “The Perceptron: A Probabilistic Model for Information
Storage and Organization in the Brain.” *Psychological Review*, 65(6),
386–408 (1958). [PubMed citation record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13602029/);
[original article copy](https://web.stanford.edu/class/psych209a/ReadingsByDate/01_30/Rosenblatt58Perceptron.pdf).
DOI: [10.1037/h0042519](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0042519).

## Analysis

- **Established — question and model:** Rosenblatt analyzes a perceptron as a
  probabilistic model for forming associations between stimuli and responses.
  The article discusses modifiable connections and how learning depends on
  the structure of the stimuli presented.
- **Established — generalization:** In the paper's conclusions, a random
  environment gives no basis for generalization, while a differentiated
  environment with similar examples associated to the same responses can
  support better-than-chance recognition of unseen examples.
- **Established — stated limitation:** The paper says the perceptron has
  difficulty with relationships between stimuli and higher-order abstraction.
- **Inference — why it matters to Entropy:** This is the historical link to
  Checkpoint 04: learning a stable pattern and memorizing unrelated answers
  are different outcomes. Our synthetic experiment makes that distinction
  measurable with train and held-out accuracy.
- **Inference — limitation for this project:** Rosenblatt's perceptron is not
  the same algorithm as Entropy's multilayer network trained with
  backpropagation. The paper's biological framing should not be read as proof
  that our small model reproduces biological learning.
