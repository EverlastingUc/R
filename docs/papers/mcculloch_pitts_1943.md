# McCulloch and Pitts (1943)

## Citation

Warren S. McCulloch and Walter Pitts. “A logical calculus of the ideas
immanent in nervous activity.” *Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics*, 5,
115–133 (1943). [Publisher record and abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02478259).

## Analysis

- **Established — question and model:** The authors give a formal account of
  idealized all-or-none neural activity using propositional logic. They state
  that the behavior of a net can be described in logical terms and that, under
  specified conditions, logical expressions can be realized by a net.
- **Established — assumptions:** Their formal treatment makes explicit
  assumptions about fixed thresholds, timing, inhibition, and an unchanging
  network structure. The paper itself says this formal equivalence is not a
  factual explanation of the biological processes behind learning.
- **Inference — why it matters to Entropy:** It provides historical context for
  representing a network as connected units that combine signals. Entropy's
  neuron uses continuous weights and a sigmoid; it is not a direct recreation
  of the paper's binary, timed model.
- **Inference — limitation for this project:** This paper does not provide the
  gradient-descent training loop Entropy uses. It is a logical model of
  networks, not a practical recipe for training our classifier.
